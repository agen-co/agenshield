AI coding agents run commands, read files, and reach the internet on behalf of a
developer — at machine speed, often without anyone watching. AgenShield puts a
boundary around them: it **finds every agent**, applies **policy your security
team defines centrally** to what each may run, read, and reach, and **records
every decision**.

Developers keep using Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex the way they already do.

## The idea in one page

<Steps>
  <Step title="AgenShield finds the AI agents on the Mac">
    Installed agents are detected automatically and listed as **targets**. Each
    one is either protected or not.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You protect an agent">
    Protecting an agent registers it with the security extensions. From then
    on, everything it runs, reads, and connects to passes through AgenShield
    first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Policy arrives from your organization">
    The Mac receives a signed policy from your AgenShield console and applies it
    locally — including while offline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You start by watching, not blocking">
    In **monitor** mode nothing is blocked; activity is simply recorded. That
    evidence is what tells you which rules are worth enforcing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You promote rules to enforcement one at a time">
    Turn on blocking for a specific rule once you have seen the real traffic —
    never fleet-wide on day one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That loop — **observe, decide, enforce, repeat** — is the whole product.

## Good to know up front

- **Policy is managed centrally.** There is no local override on the device — a
  developer cannot turn enforcement off. Changes go through your AgenShield
  console.
- **Protection survives being offline.** The last policy the Mac received stays
  in force, and activity is recorded locally and uploaded when connectivity
  returns.
- **It cannot lock you out of your Mac.** The limits that protect your own
  account and macOS itself are built into the signed security extension and are
  not configurable — not by policy, not by an administrator, not by anyone with
  access to the device.
- **macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon.** Intel Macs are not supported yet. Intel
  and Linux support are in development. Windows has a one-line install today
  (see [Install and uninstall](/getting-started/install-and-uninstall)), but
  enforcement is not yet at full parity with macOS — see
  [Windows install warnings](/troubleshoot/windows-install-warnings) for two
  known limitations (an unsigned installer that triggers a Windows SmartScreen
  warning, and the command-line enrollment token method — not the install link
  or a Group Policy/Intune push — not completing on non-English Windows
  editions).
